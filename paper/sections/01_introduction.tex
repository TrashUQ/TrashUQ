Waste mismanagement poses critical environmental and public health
challenges. Intelligent waste sorting systems powered by deep learning can
significantly improve recycling rates, but conventional approaches rely on
centralized cloud infrastructure that raises privacy, latency, and bandwidth
concerns~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}. Federated Learning (FL) addresses these
limitations by training models collaboratively across edge devices while
keeping data local~\cite{li2020fedavg,kairouz2021fl}.

Recent works have shown mature FL methods and system-level considerations for heterogeneous environments~\cite{kairouz2021fl}. However, most existing
solutions focus on simulation-based evaluation and lack validation on real
heterogeneous hardware. The availability of low-cost edge AI platforms such as
the Arduino UNO Q---featuring a Qualcomm QRB2210 processor and an STM32U585
co-processor---enables practical FL deployment on physical devices.

This paper presents TrashUQ, a complete FL-based waste monitoring system
deployed on two Arduino UNO Q boards with heterogeneous roles. Our
contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A real FL deployment on two Arduino UNO Q boards, including a
  camera-equipped capture-and-classify node and a compute-only FL participant,
  with multi-node telemetry validation and FL round-trip timing.
  \item FL simulation results on a controlled synthetic TrashNet-style dataset
  with FedAvg across 2, 5, 10, and 20 non-IID clients, demonstrating
  convergence behavior and communication efficiency.
  \item An open-source implementation of the complete FL pipeline, from
  on-device fine-tuning to MQTT telemetry, publicly available at
  \url{https://github.com/TrashUQ/TrashUQ}~\cite{trashuqrepo}.
\end{enumerate}
